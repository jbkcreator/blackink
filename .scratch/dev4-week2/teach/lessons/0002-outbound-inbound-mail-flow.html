The Blackink mail loop

Outbound sends. Inbound catches the reply. Together they're one loop — this is how it flows.

Mental model: Blackink is a two-way conversation engine. Outbound pushes cold/sequence emails out through tenant-isolated domains. Inbound catches everything that comes back — replies, and fresh owner inquiries — and decides what happens next. The two share the same contacts, the same events log, and the same rule: anything inbound can stop outbound.

BUILT outbound exists today. PLANNED inbound (Task 4.2) is greenfield — designed in the wayfinder map, not yet coded.

How they connect — the loop closes

The join points are three shared things: the contact, the events log, and the stop signal. Watch what an inbound reply does to an outbound sequence:

outbound touch sent ─────────────▶ prospect │ replies / books / opts out ▼ INBOUND catches it │ ┌───────────────────┼───────────────────┐ ▼ ▼ ▼ reply logged meeting_booked is_opted_out │ │ │ └─────────▶ HALT the sequence ◀────────┘ (no more outbound touches to this contact)
The golden rule: inbound always wins over outbound. A reply, a booking, or an opt-out immediately stops the cold/cadence touches. This is true for the outbound sequence today and must hold for the Speed-to-Lead six-attempt cadence (map ticket 10).
